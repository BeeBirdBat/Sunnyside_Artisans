Dunbar Communities represent a long-term, evolutionary vision for how we live, work, and relate to the Earth. As Sunnyside Artisans, we recognize that our current systems are failing to prioritize the long-term health of our environment and people. We are living in a time of systemic fragility, where our current way of life—built on immense scale, complexity, ecological extraction, and hierarchical power—often functions at the expense of our health and the prosperity of future generations. If we care about prosperity beyond our own lifetime, we must explore new human-made systems.  We believe that if we design for the environment and the creatures we live among, we naturally design for ourselves. The Dunbar Community is our answer: a regenerative, land-based model designed at a human scale to foster true democracy, ecological stewardship, and social capital.  

One approach to facilitate this might be by way of a land trust. There may be somebody out there who has knowledge of doing this and/or is interested in researching this and developing this as a possible avenue for managing Sunnyside Artisans Dunbar communities.

The core of this vision is Dunbar’s Number—the approximate theoretical limit to the number of people with whom one can maintain stable social relationships. By capping our communities at this size, we unlock three critical advantages. First, we foster true democracy, where members can stay authentically informed, and decision-making becomes participatory rather than hierarchical, replacing "Power Over" with "Power Within." Second, we build social capital, where trust becomes the primary currency and the small-network effect ensures accountability and mutual support. Third, we establish a human pace, where everything essential for daily existence—food, shelter, and artisanal economy —is reachable within a several-minute walk, eliminating the need for costly transportation. In this model, we will use vehicles appropriate to the scale of the community, such as bikes, e-bikes, and other electric transportation suitable for moving materials.  These communities are designed to fit within their local water cycles, integrating community-scale technology and machinery to match the scale of the group—from Fab Labs and community agriculture to transportation. This creates an "Artisanal Metabolism," where we reintegrate life and labor, replacing the "Invisible Hand" with the "Engaged Hand." 

Furthermore, our growth follows the principle of division: when a community matures, it does not grow larger but instead splits like a honeybee hive, growing through replication and adaptation to create a federation of distinct, bioregional based communities.
We recognize that we are not islands, but part of a wider network. A goal is to establish Dunbar Communities across areas such that it would be possible to ride a bike to another Dunbar Community within a day.  Each community could support and aid each other in this way.  Many possible activities could be realized from such a configuration.  This could even create a corridor for high-quality, low-impact bike tourism, offering an alternative to the extractive "vacation" industry as well as a method for material, social, and educational transfer between communities.
